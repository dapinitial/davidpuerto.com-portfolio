What I am here to do

Grow the next people who build the thing before it exists.

A pawn becomes anything if it gets far enough up the board. I have been the pawn in six chapters. The seventh is about the people behind me: giving them the route, the systems, and a standard that holds without me in the room.

Since Microsoft

At Apple I collapsed fourteen executive dashboards into Project Studio, built the ingestion pipelines that rebuild thousands of hand-tuned charts one to one, and turned the surface into SuperComposer, a publishing system forty-plus teams run themselves with Editor and Approver roles built in.

Alongside it I run a studio practice. Unakin is where the hard things get tried first: a client portal gated by row-level security, shipmate, a deploy tool that refuses to execute unless a plan ran in the last ten minutes, and the cursor lab, where every input modality is an adapter over one bus. Spacelab Studio is the production side of that practice.

And I wrote the playbook: the fourth generation of the Preview Site, where a mockup becomes a gated preview, the design language is data, and the judgment lives in 118 rules with checks. It is the thing I would hand a distinguished engineer on day one and say, finesse this.

What design means to me

Design is a decision about someone else's time, made visible early enough to be cheap to change.

Everything I have built is that sentence. The Preview Site made a PM's decision visible before engineering spent a sprint. The iViz wheel made a surgeon's one-handed grip the constraint before hardware was cut. The playbook's gate makes a design-system violation visible before a reviewer's afternoon is spent on it.

How I have seen design teams work best

One shared artifact beats shared opinions. The 2013 Experience Team at SonoSite put industrial design, interaction design, human factors, mechanical engineering and one software engineer around one prototype. The arguments were with the thing.

Judgment lives in the system, not in whoever is in the room. The playbook's 118 rules and 473 tests. Apple's test harness on the design system. Quality that survives a reorg.

Short loops with real users, with receipts. Lyric's telemetry plus Facebook Groups feedback. iViz A/B on camera first, then with SonoSite users and non-users.

Clear roles let people run in parallel. SuperComposer: editors draft, approvers sign, schedulers deliver, in sandboxed collections so seven lines of business never cross.

Make being wrong cheap. Plan before execute. Per-change undo. Drafts gated by row-level security. Teams take risks when rollback is one step.

How I get adoption across teams and orgs

Ship the thing they already need and put the system inside it. SuperComposer makes the branded email a team needs today; the design system rides along. Forty-plus teams, no mandate.

Lower the switching cost to near zero. Project Studio onboarded the existing charts one to one, so moving off Tableau felt safe. Preview Sites ran on corpnet with synthetic data, so engineers could open them without a ticket.

Give receipts, not promises. Weekly executive reporting from twelve hours to under twenty minutes. Nordstrom task completion from sixty to a hundred percent. A forensic agent surfacing data bugs before the C-suite saw them.

Make it self-serve, then hand it off with a named owner. Guest passes. Installable skills. A Chrome extension. Documentation that outlived my involvement at WebMD. Adoption is a count of other people, and the count is the receipt.

What I am here to do today

Lead design engineering in an AI-native world. That means the design language is data, the judgment is held in tests, and agents get adopted because they are contained, not because they are capable. Capability is the easy half.

And grow the next people who do it. I have sat in the PM chair, the designer's chair, the engineer's chair and the security review, so I can translate between them and I can teach the translation. The standard should travel without me in the room. That is the whole job.
